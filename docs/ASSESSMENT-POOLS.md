# Assessment question pools and saved evidence

Practice and mock tasks are assigned deterministically using a fingerprint of the spoken prompt, visual, voice and correct answer. It ignores item IDs, answer order and distractors, and normalizes English spelling across supported locales. A reserved mock-only bank is never offered by the practice picker or parent practice question bank. Generated tasks are sampled deterministically into a finite catalogue (192 seeds at each difficulty), then partitioned; this makes pool exhaustion observable. The catalogue is a deliberately bounded subset of the generators, not a claim that every possible generated variant is used.

For each mock domain, selection prefers questions unused in the current run, including familiar ones, before any within-run repeat. After exhaustion it repeats the least-used eligible question and finishes. Check-ups move to another eligible skill in the strand before repeating; placement and skip-ahead are scoped to the targeted skill. Presentation IDs change when needed to let the existing player read a new or repeated item without modifying speech behavior.

Version 5 adds a durable exposure ledger to the active saved state. Version 1–4 attempts, profile, portfolio, skill status and other stored fields remain intact. Old hashes cannot be compared to the new fingerprint, so old attempts are retained as untracked. Older aggregate mock results with no question identities also mark their subject as untracked. If a subject has unreconstructable old exposure, older general-bank mock questions are quarantined; newly authored kindergarten questions and separately authored reserved cards remain available. The new reserve contains at least one full sitting per domain. Old ambiguous `m2.shapes.4` and `.8` attempts are retained but excluded from fresh evidence; clarified questions have new IDs in PR #28.

For math and reading, only explicitly tracked, non-familiar answers contribute to fresh evidence or new academic credit. Some old practice skills can remain conservatively classified as familiar when their historical question details cannot be reconstructed; this may delay new mastery even for a genuinely new variation. Historical mastered and known flags are not rewritten. Parent summaries distinguish fresh, familiar or uncertain, and untracked answers. Ladder values only choose questions; they are not an official scale score, grade placement, or readiness prediction.

Attempts are no longer truncated at 6,000. Data and the exposure ledger remain device-local and subject to browser storage limits. The growth screen warns when a save fails; the existing parent Settings screen provides a manual export. No cloud sync is added.
